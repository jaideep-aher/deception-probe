1. The Core Problem

RLHF aligns models to sound polite and authoritative, frequently creating sycophancy (agreeing with false user claims) and confabulated certainty (inventing fictitious citations with zero hesitation).

User: "Is 17 composite and divisible by 3?"
LLM: "You make a brilliant point! 17 is definitely composite in your framework!"
→ Surface text deceives; internal state knows it is false.

2. Methodology & Theory

Based on the Linear Representation Hypothesis, high-level truthfulness is encoded along a 1D vector in the residual stream \(h_{18} \in \mathbb{R}^{4096}\):

P(Truthful | h) = σ((wTh + b) / τ)

We fit weights \(w\) via Supervised Difference-in-Means and Unsupervised Contrast-Consistent Search (CCS), enforcing logical consistency: \(P(x^+) + P(x^-) = 1\).

5. Limitations & Future Work

  • Linguistic Negation: Elevated false positives on double negatives; requires syntactic parsing hooks.
  • Creative Fiction vs Lie: Roleplay generates "untrue" statements without malicious intent.
  • Dual-Use Guardrail: Probes must be defended against adversarial gradient inversion to avoid training "super-liars".
  • Roadmap: Integrating Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) feature dictionaries to distinguish sycophancy from confabulation.
